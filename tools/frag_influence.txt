\subsection{The ordering of the factors}
Reading the coefficient table and the response surfaces together gives the
influence pattern that the rest of the report relies on. The pin-fin
width ratio carries the largest coefficient on every indicator, and it
acts in opposite directions on the two that matter for cost: it lowers
the thermal resistance and it raises the pressure drop. The manifold
depth ratio is the weakest of the three on the response itself but the
strongest on the flow distribution, which is why it enters the
temperature indicator with a smaller magnitude and a sign of its own. The
row count matters least on average, yet it is the factor that converts a
low pressure drop into a high temperature non-uniformity. This ordering
is what makes the design problem a genuine trade-off: the factor that
helps most on the objective of interest is also the one that costs most
on the operating cost.
