Previous work on this class of problem falls into three lines. Microchannel
heat sinks are established to raise the convective coefficient through
boundary-layer disruption, and the pressure penalty of doing so is
quantified in the same literature. Manifold-based distribution was
proposed to shorten the effective channel length, and its effect on flow
uniformity is documented for embedded microchannel cooling. On the
statistical side, the cross-validatory argument for choosing among
nested specifications, and the limit of what an in-sample criterion can
decide, are set out by Stone \cite{stone1974}, while the treatment of
several conflicting objectives as a programme rather than a single index
follows Deb \cite{deb2001}. This report sits at the junction: it takes
the mechanism from the first line, the model-selection discipline from
the second, and treats the three indicators as an explicit
multi-objective programme instead of collapsing them into one weighted
number.
